Where it lives: retrospective first, run-time later

The four signals you listed share one crucial property: none of them need a model forecast. Volume, wicks, taker ratio, trade counts are all raw kline columns already in your DB. That means swing detection can be computed after the fact for any coin/timeframe/date-range — including every simulation you've already run. So:

Phase 1 — a "Swings" tab on the completed-sim detail pane (next to Backtest). Same pattern as the Backtest tab: a GET /kline-simulations/{id}/swings endpoint that pulls the run's klines, computes the signal battery with trailing-only normalization (no lookahead), combines them into a composite crest score, applies an entry rule with live knobs (threshold, long/short, fee bps, hold time), and returns the standard fee-aware equity curve vs buy-and-hold. The simulation record is just the anchor — it supplies coin/pair/timeframe/range and lets you compare "signal-only" against "model forecast" against "signal + model confirmation" (the sim's prob_up as an extra filter) on the same chart. Iteration is instant: change a threshold, the curve re-renders, no re-running anything.

Phase 2 — a run-time strategy target, only once phase 1 shows net-of-fees promise. Two variants, in order of ambition: feed the signals as covariates to Chronos-2 (the sim path currently passes covariates=None; Chronos-2 natively accepts them), or add a "swing" strategy that walk-forwards a swing-proximity label. These must be run-time because walk-forward forecasts are generated bar by bar. This is where an AI model gets the chance to find interactions the hand-built composite misses.

The one thing to keep sacred in phase 1: entry signals use only past data; swing labels (extreme of ±12 bars) are hindsight and are used only for diagnostics — the monetization verdict comes from forward returns after the signal fires, not from "did we label a swing correctly."

Your four signals, plus what I'd add

Realization considerations that will make or break it

- Few knobs, standardized signals. Every signal as a z-score vs its trailing window, equal-weighted composite, one entry threshold. Ten hand-tuned thresholds on a retrospective tab is an overfitting machine — the Sig-column lesson applies doubly here because you'll be iterating interactively.

- Out-of-sample discipline. Tune knobs on one date range, confirm on a later untouched range before believing anything. The tab could enforce this softly (show the metric split by first/second half of the range).

- The verdict metric is net bps per trade after fees, not swing-detection hit rate (base rates make hit rates flattering). Reuse the equity/Sharpe/drawdown framework, plus a significance t on the forward-return edge.

- Fees decide viability. The measured edges (3–7 bps/bar fades, +7–26 bps over 6 bars) die at spot taker fees and live at futures maker fees. Fading into a turn is limit-order-friendly, so maker fills are realistic — the tab should default to a maker-fee preset (~2–4 bps/side) with taker as comparison.

- Exits matter as much as entries. Start with a fixed H-bar hold (mirrors your new horizon scoring, keeps stats clean); opposite-signal and trailing exits are v2 knobs.

- Asymmetry. Bottoms signaled more strongly than tops in every dataset (capitulation is louder than euphoria) — build long-only / short-only / both as a toggle and expect long-at-bottoms to carry the results.
